## The song's central promise
A punk protest anthem built on one image: flying the flag upside down — the official distress signal — over the whole country. The chorus chant is designed for a room to yell.

## Strongest existing element — DO NOT TOUCH
- The chorus inversion: *"They say it's the land of the brave / but now just home of the enslaved."* That couplet is the song.
- The final-chorus twist from "home of the enslaved" to *"now we're just here to enslave"* — perspective shift from victim to accusation. Keep it; consider making the vocal delivery mark the change.
- The chant simplicity of the title hook — it should stay raw, not stacked with harmonies until the last chorus.

## Weakest existing element
Verse imagery leans abstract: shadows, veils, gray skies, "nefarious groups." The song asserts distress more than it shows it. The bridge (children's plea) is the most concrete moment — the verses could take one step toward that specificity. **Proposed, not applied — Jeff's call entirely** (original preserved in the lyric doc; any edit would be shown as a diff for approval).

**What the data says about the two remaining overdubs:**
- The energy profile is nearly flat from 0:10 to 1:50 (~2dB swing). The chorus barely lifts above the verse. So "more guitars" should be treated as a **contrast** move, not a thickness move: either pull something OUT of the verses (drop a rhythm layer, tighten palm-muting) or make the chorus additions arrive as an event (doubles panned wide + octave line entering together).
